## The three doctrines that carry the product

1. **A model may parse, but never number.** The vision endpoint is contractually forbidden from
   returning calories; photo logging routes through the text analyzer so an invented macro has
   nowhere to enter a daily total. `NutrientResolver` is the only thing allowed to say what a food
   contains.
2. **Grounding, then receipts.** Every number in a coach reply is checked against evidence
   (2% relative / 1 absolute tolerance, integers <= 10 ignored). A mismatch regenerates once, then
   falls to a deterministic reply. `receipts.ts` keeps the provenance grounding would discard.
3. **Two clinical gates.** `screenForClinicalRisk()` before the model (emergency, symptom,
   diagnosis, medication, self-harm, disordered eating). `outputSafety.ts` after it (restriction,
   compensation-framing, diagnosis, body comment). Both work offline.

**F-04 — MAJOR — Apple Health / Health Connect is a seam with a null provider.**
`WearableSource` is well designed (consent-gated, local-only, cached, fail-soft) but
`nullWearableProvider` reports `unavailable` forever. Recovery scoring and the anticipation engine's
readiness folds degrade to manually typed sleep.

**F-05 — MAJOR — The AI backend lives in another repository.**
`services/api/config.ts` points at `/backend-welliva`, which is absent from this tree. Gozlin chat,
plan generation, USDA lookup and photo analysis all depend on it, with no CI coverage here. The
fail-closed check rejecting non-HTTPS and private hosts in release builds is good defensive work.
Path: pin the `/v1` response shapes in a contract test in this repo.

**F-06 — MODERATE — The README describes a different app.**
It advertises Google Gemini as the AI provider (the backend is Claude Haiku) and mentions none of
Gozlin, health-os, the fitness module, habits, the learning loop, billing tiers or the consent gate.

**F-07 — MODERATE — Accessibility coverage is partial.**

`accessibilityRole`.

**F-08 — VERIFIED CLEAN — Secrets, RLS, offline fallback.**
`.env` is untracked and gitignored; only the Supabase anon key appears in `.env.example`, which is
correct under RLS. All 12 tables have row-level security with 13 policies across 4 migrations.

seed, so a first launch with no signal still has food and diet data. No findings.

## Where Welliva is genuinely ahead

1. **Numbers you can audit.** No shipping competitor shows where a figure came from, because none
   of them can — a number a model computed has no provenance to display. Welliva forbade the model
   from computing figures, which is what makes the receipt possible. Most defensible thing here.
2. **Safety as architecture, not prompting.** Cal AI and MFP's assistant rely on system prompts.
   Prompts reduce a rate; they do not floor it. A deterministic pre-model screen plus a
   post-generation advice check is a materially different safety posture.
3. **Clinical range no consumer app attempts.** Renal, hepatic, oncology recovery, elimination
   protocols, life-stage nutrition — with per-condition hard constraints injected into the coach's
   context. The nearest real substitute is a registered dietitian.
4. **Metabolism it actually learns.** A two-state Kalman filter estimating true weight and true
   TDEE, with process noise on the TDEE term modelling adaptive thermogenesis, and missing days
   handled by skipping the update step. MacroFactor built a business on this; MFP, Lose It and Noom
   still serve a static Mifflin–St Jeor number that drifts furthest exactly when a user is deepest
   into a cut.
5. **Offline-first as a real guarantee.** Fully functional with no backend, no key and no signal.
   Noom and Cal AI are unusable offline.
6. **Privacy posture built in, not bolted on.** Per-category consent gates, local-only wearable
   reads with only a derived score surfaced, a user-facing Trust screen, real server-side erasure.
   MyFitnessPal disclosed a 150M-account breach in 2018; trust is a live differentiator here.
7. **One app instead of five.** Diet, guided training, habits, achievements and a coach that reads
   all of them. Competitors are point solutions that cannot see each other.

## Where the incumbents are better

1. **Logging a real packaged meal.** Decisive and not close. Two-second barcode scan in MFP vs.
   search 205 foods, miss, trigger a network lookup. Logging *is* the core loop of a food tracker.
2. **Data breadth from crowdsourcing.** MFP's ~20M entries are often wrong and Welliva's curation is
   defensibly better per entry — but a wrong entry a user can correct beats a missing one they
   cannot log at all, and users experience the missing entry as the app's fault.
3. **Passive data capture.** Steps, smart-scale weight, watch workouts, ring sleep. Whoop and Oura
   built products on the insight that the best log is one nobody has to write. Every Welliva input
   is manual.
4. **Barbell training depth.** Hevy, Strong and Fitbod track per-set weight and reps with full
   history, PR detection, plate math and progressive-overload suggestions. The time-based session
   HUD is excellent for bodyweight and HIIT and a deliberate choice, but a serious lifter cannot use
   this as their training log.
5. **Social retention loops.** MFP friends, Strava kudos, Hevy's feed, Noom's groups. Accountability
   from other humans is the strongest retention mechanic in the category. Building it conflicts with
   the privacy posture — a real strategic question — but the retention cost is real either way.
6. **Photo logging that actually ships.** Cal AI's whole product is point-camera-at-plate and it
   grew fast on exactly that. Welliva's version is architecturally better (it refuses to invent
   macros) but is gated behind a backend not in this repository, so today it degrades to text.
7. **Distribution and trust-by-default.** Superior architecture does not overcome a distribution
   deficit on its own.

## Verdict

The engineering here is better than the engineering at most funded competitors in this category —
the comment density, the stated doctrines, the test coverage and the fail-soft seams are the work of
someone building for the long term.

The strategic risk is not quality. It is that **the hard, defensible work is done and the easy,
decisive work isn't.** A Kalman-filtered TDEE estimator and a two-layer clinical safety architecture
would take a competitor a year to replicate. Barcode scanning is a week. Right now a user who cannot
log their lunch will churn before they ever see the filter converge.

Ship the week of work. Then the year of work has someone to serve.
